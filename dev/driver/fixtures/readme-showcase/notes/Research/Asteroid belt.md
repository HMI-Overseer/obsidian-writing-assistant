# Asteroid belt

Notes from the Wikipedia article. The numbers are the article's.

## Where, and how much

- Between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. The main belt runs from about 2.06 to 3.27 AU.
- Total mass about 2.39 x 10^21 kg, which is 3% of the mass of the Moon.
- Ceres alone is 39% of that. Ceres, Vesta, Pallas and Hygiea together hold about 62%.
- Between 700,000 and 1.7 million asteroids of 1 km or more. Over 200 are larger than 100 km.

## How empty it is

- On average the distance between asteroids is about 965,600 km.
- Probes have crossed it many times. The odds of a probe hitting an asteroid are put at less than 1 in a billion.

## Structure

- Kirkwood gaps: orbits whose period is an integer fraction of Jupiter's get swept clear. The 4:1 resonance at 2.06 AU and the 2:1 at 3.27 AU mark the edges.
- About a third of belt asteroids belong to a collisional family.
- Collisions between bodies of about 10 km radius happen roughly once every 10 million years.

## Composition and conditions

- C-type (carbonaceous) make up over 75% of visible asteroids. S-type (silicate) about 17%. The rest are mostly X-type, the metal-rich ones among them.
- Temperatures from about 200 K (minus 73 C) at 2.2 AU down to 165 K (minus 108 C) at 3.2 AU.
- Simulations suggest the original belt held about an Earth's worth of mass. Jupiter's pull made shattering win over accretion, which is why there is no planet.
