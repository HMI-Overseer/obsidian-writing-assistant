# Rings of Saturn

Notes from the Wikipedia article. The numbers are the article's.

## Shape

- The dense main rings extend from 7,000 km to 80,000 km above Saturn's equator.
- Local thickness as little as 10 m and as much as 1 km. A sheet, not a cloud.
- Outward: D, C, B, A, then F, G and E.
- The Cassini Division between the B and A rings is 4,800 km wide. Particles there would orbit twice for every orbit of Mimas, so Mimas keeps it clear.

## What they are made of

- 99.9% water ice with a smattering of impurities.
- Mostly particles smaller than 10 m, down to dust.
- Total mass about 1.54 x 10^19 kg, roughly 0.41 times the mass of Mimas.

## Gaps and moons

- Pan clears the Encke Gap in the A ring. Daphnis orbits inside the Keeler Gap, near the A ring's outer edge.
- Prometheus and Pandora shepherd the F ring.
- Over 150 "propeller" moonlets had been found in the A ring by 2008.

## Odd facts to use

- Spokes in the B ring: radial features that turn with the planet rather than with the ring, which orbital mechanics alone cannot explain.
- The rings may be only 10 to 100 million years old, and could be gone in 300 million.
- Cassini's Grand Finale in 2017 measured 4,800 to 44,000 kg/s of material falling from the rings into Saturn. Ring rain.
